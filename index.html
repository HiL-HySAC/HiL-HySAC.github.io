Abstract

Abstract

Real-world online reinforcement learning bypasses the sim-to-real gap, but existing human-in-the-loop methods often explore small workspaces and struggle when one policy must learn continuous robot motion alongside discrete gripper actions. Our CTDE framework decouples Cartesian pose and gripper control into two actor-agents while an omniscient joint critic coordinates their training. A multi-head hybrid reward architecture decomposes the learning target, and a worst-case constraint promotes safe exploration. Across robotic-arm and humanoid experiments, the method learns robust manipulation under randomization zones approximately ten times larger than prior work with minimal human supervision.

Method

Method

Hybrid control is separated at execution and coordinated during training through shared value estimation.

01

Decentralized actors

One actor controls the continuous Cartesian pose while a second actor controls discrete gripper actions, avoiding the instability of a single hybrid-action policy.

02

Centralized critic

An omniscient joint critic observes both agents during training, coordinating their learning while preserving independent, decentralized execution.

03

Reward decomposition

A multi-head hybrid reward architecture learns simpler sub-targets. The grasp-head Q-value reshapes the actor loss, improving sample efficiency and convergence.
